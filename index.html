A free field map for walkers

Your guide to Epping Forest.
No signal needed.

An offline map of all 24,906 veteran trees in the forest. Type the number on a tree’s tag to see its record, its estimated age and a way there. Paths, pubs, car parks and the grazing cattle are on it too.

Find the tree behind the tag

Veteran trees are the forest’s oldest and most valued trees, many of them pollards hundreds of years old. The Veteran Tree Register records them, and the small numbered tags you’ll see on many trunks link a tree to its record.

Look up a tree you’ve spotted, or pick one to find on your next walk.

Works where your phone doesn't

Download the map once, over wi-fi or signal, and it lives on your device. Walk into the middle of the forest and every tree, path and landmark is still there.

Follow the longhorns

Epping Forest's grazing cattle wear GPS collars, and the map shows where they were last reported. Cows wander, so while you have signal the app checks for new positions every so often. Offline, you’ll see the last saved positions.

Everything else out there

Paths and bridleways, pubs and cafés, car parks, benches, toilets and gates, bus stops and stations. All within walking distance of the forest, and all of it offline.

How it works offline

One download on signal, then the whole forest travels with you.

  1. Open it onceSomewhere with signal or wi-fi.
  2. It downloadsThe whole forest saves to your device — a one-time wait.
  3. Walk out of rangeThe map, your position and your heading all keep working.

Questions

When can I start using it?
Epping Forest Finds is in a closed alpha, and invitations aren’t open yet. Leave your email and you’ll hear as soon as they are.
Does it really work without a phone signal?
Yes. Once downloaded, the map, trees and paths work offline. Fresh cow locations need a connection; offline, the map shows their last saved positions.
Do I need to install anything?
No. It runs in your phone’s web browser on iPhone and Android, so there is nothing to find in an app store. You can add it to your home screen if you like.
Is it free?
Yes, it is free to use and there is no account to create.
Where does the tree data come from?
The Epping Forest Veteran Tree Register. Paths, pubs, car parks and transport come from OpenStreetMap contributors.
How do you know where the cattle are?
The forest's grazing cattle wear GPS collars, and their positions are published through the Nofence open data API. The map reads that feed when it loads and periodically makes a network request to refresh it, because the cows move around. Offline, you see their last saved positions, which may be out of date.
Does it track me?
Only if you say yes. The app asks before sharing your location. If you agree, it sends anonymous location and usage data that helps improve the map, and you can turn it off at any time in Settings. A mailing-list address is never linked to it. See the privacy notice.
Which area does it cover?
Epping Forest and a walking-distance band around its boundary, so the pub or station just outside the treeline is on the map too.
