A free, offline map of Epping Forest — 24,906 veteran trees, ready to discover. Find a tree by its tag number, navigate to it and see its estimated age. Paths, pubs, facilities and grazing cattle are on the map too.

Find the tree behind the tag

Those numbered tags connect the trees around you to the Veteran Tree Register. Look up a tree you’ve spotted, or choose a specific one to find on your next walk.

  1. Search its tag number. Enter the number on the tree’s physical tag to find its record on the map.
  2. Navigate to that tree. Select it to see the way from your location, then check its tag number when you arrive.
  3. Discover its estimated age. We use the recorded girth and species to make an educated guess, where the data is available — not an exact birthday.

Works where your phone doesn't

Download the map once, over wi-fi or signal. After that it is on your device: every tree, path and landmark, with no network needed. Walk into the middle of the forest and it keeps working.

Every veteran tree in the register

All 24,906 trees from the Epping Forest Veteran Tree Register, each one placed on the map with its species and record. Search by the number on a tree’s physical tag to find that specific tree and explore its estimated age.

Follow the longhorns

Epping Forest's grazing cattle wear GPS collars. The map shows their latest reported positions. Cows move around, so the app periodically makes a network request for up-to-date locations. Offline, you’ll see the last saved positions.

How it works offline

  1. Open it once somewhere with signal or wi-fi.
  2. It downloads the whole forest to your device — a one-time wait.
  3. Walk out of range. The map, your position and your heading all keep working.

Fresh cow locations need a connection. The app periodically requests updates because the cows move around; offline positions may be out of date.

Questions

Does it really work without a phone signal?
Yes. Once downloaded, the map, trees and paths work offline. Fresh cow locations need a connection; offline, the map shows their last saved positions.
Is it free?
Yes, it is free to use and there is no account to create.
Where does the tree data come from?
The Epping Forest Veteran Tree Register. Paths, pubs, car parks and transport come from OpenStreetMap contributors.
How do you know where the cattle are?
The forest's grazing cattle wear GPS collars, and their positions are published through the Nofence open data API. The map reads that feed when it loads and periodically makes a network request to refresh it, because the cows move around. Offline, you see their last saved positions, which may be out of date.
Does it drain my battery?
The map limits redrawing to reduce battery use. Your screen and location services still use power, and the app periodically makes network requests to refresh cow locations.
Which area does it cover?
Epping Forest and a walking-distance band around its boundary, so the pub or station just outside the treeline is on the map too.
