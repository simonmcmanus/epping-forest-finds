How community checks work

The map is only as good as the data behind it, and people walking the forest can see things the data can’t. Community checks let you confirm what’s right, flag what’s wrong and add what’s missing. This page explains what happens to your reports.

The short version

  • The City of London’s Veteran Tree Register stays the record. Your reports sit alongside it and never overwrite it.
  • When two or more different people agree a tree is wrong, it shows a “Questioned by the community” note. It stays on the map.
  • Changes to places such as pubs, cafés and shops are always checked by a person before anything is touched.
  • We collect what people find and pass it on to the City of London so the register can be corrected at source. The current findings are public in the discrepancy report.
  • You earn points. No account is needed.

Checking a tree

Open any tree in the app and use Help check this tree. Standing next to it, you can say:

ReportMeaning
It’s here, and the tag matchesThe tree is where the map says, with the right tag.
No tag, but it looks rightA tree is here but has no tag, and it looks like the one listed.
No tag, and I doubt itNo tag, and it doesn’t look like the tree listed.
A different tree is hereA different tree stands at this spot.
There’s no tree hereNothing is at the reported location.
Tag found, map is wrongYou’re at the tag, but the map shows it somewhere else. We record where you’re standing.

Your own report shows straight away. Other people see the result after the next daily update, so it can take up to a day.

Adding a missing tree

Found a tagged tree that isn’t on the map? On the Community screen, choose Add a missing tree. You’ll need:

  • Its tag number, printed on the tag. This is what identifies the tree.
  • To be standing right next to it with a good GPS fix (accurate to 30 metres or better), because your position becomes the tree’s position. The form shows the current accuracy and won’t submit until it’s good enough.

If the tag is already on the map, we’ll point you to that tree instead. A new tree is shown to everyone once a second person has found it too.

Checking a place

Pubs, cafés, shops and other places have a Help check this place card at the bottom of their screen. You can confirm a place is still right, or say it has closed, moved, changed name, has wrong details or is the wrong type of place.

Reports about places are never applied automatically. Each change becomes a report that a person reviews, and further reports about the same place are added to it.

What happens to the reports

  1. You report from the app. If you’re offline, it’s saved on your device and sent when you’re back online.
  2. Reports from different people are compared once a day. The same device repeating itself counts once, and reports made from far away from the tree don’t count.
  3. Where people agree, the tree is marked. Where they disagree, it’s held for a person to look at.
  4. We pass confirmed differences to the City of London and ask for a corrected dataset. Once a new dataset arrives, resolved notes are cleared.

Points

  • 1 point when you verify a tree, and 1 more when someone else independently confirms it.
  • A new tree earns 1 point, and 3 more once a second person finds it.
  • A correction that others agree with earns 2 points.

Points build up through four ranks: Seedling, Sapling, Woodward and Warden. Your score and a log of everything you’ve reported are on the Community screen. Scores are private for now.

Privacy

You don’t need an account. Your reports are tied to a random identifier stored on your device, which you can clear at any time by clearing the site’s data. Your location is used to check you’re close to what you’re reporting. See the privacy and terms page for the full details.
